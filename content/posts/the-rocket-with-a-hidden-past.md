On **July 16, 1969**, a giant white rocket called the Saturn V lifted off in Florida. It carried three men to the Moon. Crowds cheered. Some cried.

Now go back about 25 years, to a hill in the middle of Germany.

## The factory inside the hill

Inside that hill, near the town of Nordhausen, the Nazis built a factory in tunnels. Prisoners dug the tunnels and built the rockets. Many of them never saw the sun for weeks.

The rocket was the **V-2**, the world's first long-range guided missile.

Historians at the Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum have studied the numbers:

- The V-2 killed a bit over **5,000** people in its attacks.
- About **60,000** prisoners were sent into the camp system around the hill.
- More than **20,000** of them are thought to have died there or on the marches out of it.
- So at least twice as many people died building the V-2 as were killed by it.

## The link most people never heard about

Some of the men who ran that rocket work were later part of the team that built the Moon rocket.

Arthur Rudolph ran day-to-day production at the underground V-2 factory. From 1963 to 1968, he ran the Saturn V program office at NASA. In 1984, after U.S. investigators looked into his past, he left the country and gave up his American citizenship. His friends still argue that he was treated unfairly. The book lays out both sides.

## Why "Paperclip"?

After the war, U.S. officers went through the files of German scientists. They clipped a paperclip to the folders of the men they wanted most. A paperclip meant: this one, bring him to America.

That simple office tool gave its name to one of the biggest secrets of the Cold War.
