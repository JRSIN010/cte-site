The Declassified Reading List

Where to read real government files for free.

FREE COPY

You do not have to trust anyone's summary, including mine. These libraries are free and open to all.

  • National Archives: JFK Recordsarchives.gov/jfkThe official home of the JFK assassination files, with the newest releases.
  • FBI Vaultvault.fbi.govThe FBI's public reading room. Files on famous people, cases and events.
  • CIA Reading Roomcia.gov/readingroomMillions of pages of released CIA papers that you can search by word.
  • National Security Archivensarchive.gwu.eduA university library that wins the release of secret files and explains them.
  • AAROaaro.milThe Pentagon office for UAP reports. It posts its reports and case videos.
  • Mary Ferrell Foundationmaryferrell.orgA free library where you can search JFK, RFK and MLK files.
  • Reuther Library: Hoffa FBI Filesreuther.wayne.eduFBI papers on the Jimmy Hoffa case, kept at Wayne State University.
  • GovInfogovinfo.govOfficial reports from Congress and federal agencies.
  • FOIA.govfoia.govAsk the government for a record yourself. Anyone can file a request.

How to Read a File

Six steps so you do not get lost.

  1. Find the date. Was it written before the event, or after? That changes what it can prove.
  2. Find who wrote it and who got it. A report to the boss is not the same as a note from a tipster.
  3. Ask what kind of paper it is. A rumor that got written down is still a rumor. A sworn statement is stronger.
  4. Look at what is blacked out. Note where the gaps are. Do not fill them in with a guess.
  5. Read the whole page. One line pulled out alone can say the opposite of the full page.
  6. Check it against a second file. One paper is a clue. Two that match are the start of proof.

Words you will see

Confidential, Secret, Top Secret
The three U.S. secrecy levels, from lowest to highest.
Declassified
The secrecy label was taken off. The public may now read it.
Redacted
Part of the page is blacked out or covered.
FOIA
The Freedom of Information Act. The law that lets you ask for records.
Released in part
You get the page, but with some of it still hidden.
Informant
A person who gave information. It may or may not be true.
ONE RULE TO KEEP: a file tells you what someone wrote down. It does not always tell you what happened. Your job is to work out the gap between the two.
